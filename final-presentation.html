Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier |
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

Justification

Autonomy Tiers: Ingest raw text data, cross-reference sources, draft specifications, and structure ideas into standard templates without asking.

Tiered Safeguards: Because it cannot push code or modify live task trackers, its potential blast radius is low. However, high-impact decisions (such as pausing executive AI initiatives or committing to enterprise delivery dates like the Okta SSO deadline) require explicit human-in-the-loop sign-off.

Binding Commitment Restrictions: Juno is strictly forbidden from outputting definitive external delivery dates or financial commitments in the "Actionable Next Steps" or "PR/FAQ" sections; all timelines must be flagged as Proposed Estimates requiring Engineering sign-off.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

PMs will be able to get to more tangible higher fidelity ideas more quickly based on the outputs of Juno AI PM

Baseline

Current Measurement: 3 man-days (24 working hours) on average to go from raw feature inputs/PRD to a complete, shareable low/mid-fidelity wireframe.

Context: Data-grounding is inconsistent; wireframe iterations often rely on human assumptions, leading to multiple rework rounds before stakeholder sign-off.

Target and review date

Primary Target:

Efficiency: Reduce hands-on ideation time by 50% (from 3 man-days down to 1.5 man-days / 12 working hours per wireframe).

Quality: > 80% of wireframe core decisions explicitly tied to quantified/qualified data inputs in initial design reviews.

Usage metric

Weekly Active Adoption (WAU): > 75% of target PMs and UX designers logging in and generating outputs at least twice a week.

Export-to-Workflow Rate: > 60% of generated Juno AI wireframe outputs exported directly into primary design/documentation tools (Figma, Miro, Notion, or Jira).

Pattern I’ll watch for first

The Signal: PMs/designers are prompting Juno AI frequently (high daily activity), but the export-to-workflow rate drops below 30%.

Meaning: Users are playing with the tool or trying to get it to work, but finding the outputs unusable for actual production wireframes, forcing them to start over manually.

Stop line

Zero Efficiency Gain + Low Adoption at 90 Days:

Criteria: If at the 90-day checkpoint, average time-to-wireframe remains above 2.5 man-days and WAU drops below 30%, use of the tool will be paused or deprecated.

Rationale: If the tool fails to save meaningful time or produce usable outputs that team members voluntarily adopt, it does not justify the operational overhead or license cost.
